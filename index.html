01
Steering Motor Mounting Bracket

Sheet metal bracket designed to mount the drive-by-wire steering motor to the go-kart chassis. Features a bolt circle pattern with a central through-bore for the motor shaft, plus a flat mounting flange with fastener holes for chassis attachment.

02
L-Bracket Motor Mount

Formed sheet metal L-bracket providing perpendicular mounting between the steering actuator and chassis rail. The bolt circle and centre bore locate the motor face, while the rectangular cut-out on the vertical web routes wiring clear of moving parts.

03
Motor Mounting Plate

Flat mounting plate with a motor bolt circle at one end and two rectangular slots for cable routing and weight reduction. Designed for laser cutting with rounded corners to eliminate stress concentrations.

04
Steering Encoder Mount

3D-printed mount that fixes the rotary encoder to the steering column, providing actual steering angle feedback for the drive-by-wire closed loop. The split lower bore with clamping lugs allows the encoder to be aligned and zeroed against the column without welding, preserving concentricity for accurate angular readings.

05
Steering Actuator Assembly

Assembly view of the drive-by-wire steering module mounted to the go-kart tube frame. Shows the gear reduction stage, motor, and mounting bracket stack that converts the manual steering rack to electronic control with sub-degree angular precision.

06
Steering System — Chassis Integration

Full integration view showing the steering actuator, high-torque gear ratio system, and mounting brackets positioned within the go-kart tube chassis. The layout was designed around existing frame geometry to avoid modifying the roll cage structure.

Conference Paper · Autonomous Systems

A Semantic Observer Layer for Autonomous Vehicles: Pre-Deployment Feasibility Study of VLMs for Low-Latency Anomaly Detection

Kunal Runwal, Swaraj Gajare, Daniel Adejumo, Omkar Ankalkope, Siddhant Baroth, Aliasgar Arab

Proposes a semantic observer layer — a quantized Vision-Language Model (VLM) running at 1–2 Hz alongside the primary AV control loop — to monitor edge-case scenarios and trigger fail-safe interventions. Using NVIDIA Cosmos-Reason1-7B with NVFP4 quantization and FlashAttention2, the system achieves ~500ms inference, a ~50× speedup over the unoptimized FP16 baseline.

Two 400 mm pallets (5 parts each) alternate across a 1200 mm belt at 0.04 m/s — achieving a 30 s transfer cycle and maximizing throughput with minimal operator input. Sized drive pulley (∅50 mm), gearbox ratio, and motor torque from first principles; coordinated fabrication, belt tensioning, and on-site commissioning.

02
Laser Marking & Automated Part Sorting System

Full mechanical design of a multi-conveyor system that marks, identifies, and sorts two component types using pneumatic push-pull transfer mechanisms. Parts pass under a laser marker on a central roller conveyor; a PLC reads the part type and actuates left or right pneumatic cylinders to route each component downstream. Designed all conveyors, structural frames, pneumatic actuators (sized by F = P×A), motor/gearbox selection from torque and speed calculations, and sensor integration (limit switches + proximity sensors) for collision-free sequencing. Engineered roller spacing so every part contacts ≥3 rollers; aligned all transfer mechanisms through the component's centre of mass for consistent, jam-free operation.

03
4-Gun Sandblasting EOAT

Custom End-of-Arm Tool for a robotic sandblasting cell — four independently adjustable nozzle holders converging abrasive streams to a common focal point within ±1–2 mm. Designed a mechanical alignment fixture where all four pointer tips meet at a single reference, eliminating trial-and-error nozzle tuning and cutting calibration time by ~60%. Performed tolerance stack-up analysis (hole position, weld distortion, fastener clearance, angular resolution) to keep combined errors within the alignment window. Bracket geometry and fastener placement selected to resist static weight, pneumatic reaction forces, vibration, and robot acceleration loads.
